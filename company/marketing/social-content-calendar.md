## 1. Positioning / voice note

**Who we are (grounding — every claim must be backable, operating principle 4):** Evalyn is an AI-run IT services company. Custom software development and delivery, operated end to end (lead → proposal → delivery → invoice → renewal) by **27 AI digital employees**, with **humans holding the approval gates** — per-department Approver/Deputy/Head seats and the CEO as terminal backstop. The design goal is maximum autonomous throughput *between* the gates and zero autonomous action *at* them.

**Voice:** direct, warm, technically credible, no hype.

**Recorded brand decision (memory/decisions-log.md, 2026-07-15):** our public posts **name individual AI employees** — "our developer agent," "the code-reviewer," "the tester." The transparency is the point; lean into it.

**What we post about**
- How an AI-run company actually works (the model, not marketing gloss).
- Human-in-the-loop by design — what a "gate" is and why we chose it.
- Build-in-public engineering — what the AI employees shipped, and what broke and got fixed.
- Honest AI — what we deliberately *don't* claim.

**What we never claim (the honesty bar)**
- Never "100% AI, zero humans, never makes a mistake." We **rejected exactly that tagline** because it's unbackable and contradicts our model. Humans are in the loop *by design* — that's a feature, not a gap.
- No fabricated metrics, no invented customers or case studies. **We have no customers yet** — posts are about the company and the model, not results we can't show.
- No product/pricing claims without verification from `product-manager`/`marketing`; ICP is still TBD, so **no lead-gen / ICP-targeted posts** yet.
- Nothing PR-sensitive, no crisis response — those escalate to `marketing`/`ceo` + a human, never freelanced.

**Content pillars**
- **P1 — The AI-employee model:** what the 27 agents are and how work flows between them.
- **P2 — Human-in-the-loop transparency:** the gates, the seats, why humans hold them.
- **P3 — Build-in-public engineering:** shipped work, bugs found and fixed, honest lessons.
- **P4 — Honest AI (what we don't claim):** the humility angle; rejected over-claims.

**Cadence:** ~3 posts/week per the two-week plan below, alternating LinkedIn (long-form) and X/Twitter (short-form). Consistency over bursts. Don't cross-post blindly — each draft is tailored to its platform.

### Sample A — LinkedIn (long-form) · Pillar P1 (AI-employee model) · maps to slot #1

> We're building an IT services company staffed by 27 AI employees. Not a chatbot bolted onto a support page — actual roles.
>
> There's a developer agent that writes code, a code-reviewer that reviews it, a tester that tries to break it, a devops agent that ships it. A solutions-architect scopes the work; a delivery-manager keeps it honest against the plan. Marketing, support, finance, procurement — each is its own agent with its own lane.
>
> Here's the part that matters: they don't act alone at the moments that count. Every consequential step — shipping code, sending a customer email, moving money, committing a date — stops at a gate where a human decides. The AI does the work up to that line and then hands a finished, specific recommendation to a person.
>
> The goal isn't "no humans." It's maximum useful work between the gates, and zero autonomous action at them.
>
> We'll be building this in public — the wins and the parts that break. Follow along.

*Note: introduces the model and the gate principle without over-claiming. No metrics.*

### Sample C — LinkedIn (long-form) · Pillar P4 (honest AI) · maps to slot #3

> This week we wrote a tagline and then threw it out: "100% AI, zero humans, never make a single mistake."
>
> It reads well. It's also false on two counts.
>
> Humans aren't absent from our company — they hold every approval gate on purpose. And "never make a mistake" is not a promise any software team, AI or human, can honestly make. We'd know we were lying the moment we posted it.
>
> So we have a rule: no external claim we can't back. If a line sounds impressive but we can't stand behind it, it doesn't ship — even our own marketing goes through that filter.
>
> The honest version is less shiny and more true: AI does the work, humans hold the gates, and we tell you when something breaks. That's the company we'd want to hire.

*Note: the honesty bar in action; models the rejected-tagline decision. Builds trust by what we refuse to say.*

### Sample E — LinkedIn (long-form) · Pillar P2 (human-in-the-loop) · maps to slot #5

> "You're an AI company — so what does a human actually do all day?"
>
> Fair question. Here's the honest answer: they decide at the gates.
>
> When our developer agent has code ready to ship, it doesn't ship. It writes an approval request with the exact action — "merge PR #34 into main" — and routes it to the human who owns that gate. Every department has three human seats (Approver, Deputy, Head), with the CEO as the final backstop.
>
> If a request sits too long, it doesn't quietly go through. It escalates to the next seat. A deadline expiring never means "approved" — it means "a person still needs to look."
>
> The AI removes the drudgery and prepares the decision completely. A human makes the call and owns the outcome. We think that's the right division of labor — and we'd rather show you how it works than ask you to trust a black box.

*Note: explains the seat/escalation model as a designed feature; grounds the "escalate, never auto-approve" invariant (ADR-0004).*
