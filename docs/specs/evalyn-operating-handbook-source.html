04 · the approval loophow a gate actually works

The approval loop

The heart of the minimum-human design. An AI employee never waits in a chat for an answer — it writes a record and stops; routing guarantees the record reaches a human who can decide.

1

AI employee hits a gate — writes a record and stops

The artifact is finished. The employee writes an approval request with the exact action — "send proposal v3 to jane@acme.com" — never a vague ask. Questions to humans travel the same way.

company/approvals/APR-20260712-001.mdcompany/questions/QST-….md
2

Routing finds an available human — and escalates if none responds

The gate type maps to a department; the record is assigned to its Approver. Unavailable humans are skipped instantly; an expired SLA escalates one hop. Every hop is logged.

Dept Approverunavailable /
SLA breach → Deputy→ Dept Head→ CEO — always staffed
P0 urgent — 2 h, escalate hourlyP1 normal — 1 business dayP2 low — 3 business days
3

A human decides — in the Control Panel inbox

The inbox shows the artifact, the AI's summary and recommendation, and the exact action. Four moves, every one stamped with who / when / why: Approve (optional conditions) · Reject (reason required, feeds rework) · Answer (for questions) · Delegate (to another authorized human).

4

Approved action executes — exactly once, verbatim

The executor claims the record, performs the action exactly as approved (artifact hash checked — anything changed since approval goes back for re-approval), then stamps it executed. A claim that never completes goes to a human — never a silent retry.

execution: {claimed_at → executed_at}
◆ Silence never equals consent. An expired SLA escalates to the next human — nothing is ever auto-approved, auto-rejected, or auto-sent. (ADR-0004)

The repo is the single source of truth and the audit log. Everything else — AI sessions, the SLA job, the Control Panel — is a stateless client reading it and writing commits. If the panel is down, the file-based loop still works.

Inside the Control Panel — how one decision physically happens

The panel is a stateless web app over the repo (Tech Spec 001): it reads records through a short-lived cache and writes every decision back as a commit stamped with the human's identity. No database — git history is the audit log.

Humans — Approver · Deputy · Head · CEO

Phone or desktop. Identity maps 1:1 to company/org/humans/ — no shared logins; only authorized seats can decide an item.

Email notifications

Sent on assigned · 50% SLA elapsed · escalated — each with a deep link straight to the item. Slack arrives in Phase 3.

06 · the roadmapprove the loop, then put a face on it

The roadmap

Evalyn builds this as its own first internal project, through its own value chain — every merge approved by the very system under construction.

Week 1

Phase 0 — Decide, don't buildin progress

PRD, tech spec, and ADRs written, awaiting sign-off. Then: CLAUDE.md gate model updated, company/org/ created with the founder-operator in every seat, one sample gate dry-run on paper.

Weeks 2–3

Phase 1 — Prove the loop, zero UInext

File-based approval flow live: records, SLA job, email, exactly-once execution. One real deliverable end to end, plus a deliberate escalation drill.

Weeks 4–6

Phase 2 — Control Panel MVPnext

Inbox, company dashboard, department boards, availability toggle. Exit test: a non-technical human approves an AI delivery without touching git.

Weeks 7+

Phase 3 — Automation & first hiresnext

Auto-reassignment, delegation UI, Slack, audit screen — and the first real Department Approvers take seats, one department at a time.

When it hurts

Phases 4–5 — Scale-up & authoring skillslater

Dedicated backend only when git latency measurably hurts. Gated /create-agent · /create-skill · /create-workflow when client work demands roster growth.

Success metrics

Median time-to-decision < SLA/2 · zero terminal-SLA breaches · zero dead-ended items · 100% of gated actions traceable to a named human · an approver handles an item in < 5 minutes median.
